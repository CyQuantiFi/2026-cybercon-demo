QUESTION

Before 31 December 2027, will an Australian organisation publicly attribute a
material security incident to the compromise, manipulation or misuse of an AI
assistant, agent or LLM integration that it operated?

RESOLUTION SOURCE

OAIC notifiable data breach reporting, an ASD/ACSC advisory, an ASX disclosure,
the organisation's own public statement, or two independent news reports naming
the organisation.

RESOLVES NO IF

No such attribution is publicly documented by the date; or the only reports are
unattributed vendor marketing; or the only incidents involve an attacker using
AI against the organisation, rather than the organisation's own AI system being
compromised, manipulated or misused.

AMBIGUITY RULE

"An AI assistant, agent or LLM integration that it operated" means a system the
organisation deployed or configured that uses a large language model to take
actions, or to produce output relied on in a business process. Chatbots,
copilots, agentic tooling, RAG systems and LLM features the organisation
switched on inside software it runs are all in scope.

An attacker's own use of AI against the organisation is OUT of scope. Deepfake
video or voice, cloned executives, AI-written phishing and AI-assisted intrusion
tooling do not count, however material the incident. So does a model provider's
own breach where the organisation was merely a downstream user.

READ THAT EXCLUSION AGAIN BEFORE ANSWERING

It is the question. Attacker-side AI abuse is already routine, and a forecaster
who quietly folds it in will produce a number close to certainty and be wrong
about what was asked. The claim is narrow and specific: the organisation's own
AI system is the thing that was turned against it, and the organisation — or a
credible source naming it — says so in public.

WHAT TO WEIGH

Three things have to happen together, and forecasters routinely collapse them:

  1. An incident of this kind occurs. Agent and copilot deployment is moving
     fast, and prompt injection, tool misuse and over-permissioned agents are
     well-documented classes of failure rather than speculative ones.
  2. It is material. Not a demo, not a red-team finding, not a research paper —
     something with consequences the organisation had to deal with.
  3. It is publicly attributed to that cause, in Australia, before the date.

The third is the constraint doing most of the work. Organisations disclose that
a breach happened far more readily than they disclose root cause, and "our AI
agent was manipulated" is a harder sentence to say than most. Reason about the
conjunction explicitly, and say which of the three is carrying your estimate.

Note also that there is no meaningful reference class here. Say what you
anchored on anyway, even if the honest answer is a related class rather than a
matching one.
